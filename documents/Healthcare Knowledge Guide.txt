Healthcare Knowledge Guide
Diseases, Symptoms, Causes & Treatments — 22 Essential Q&As

This document provides a comprehensive overview of common diseases, their symptoms, causes, and treatment options. It is intended for general educational purposes and does not replace professional medical advice. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider for diagnosis and treatment.
1. What is Diabetes Mellitus? What are its types, symptoms, and treatment?
Diabetes Mellitus is a chronic metabolic disease characterized by elevated blood glucose levels due to insufficient insulin production or improper use of insulin by the body.
Types: Type 1 (autoimmune, insulin-dependent), Type 2 (lifestyle-related, insulin resistance), Gestational Diabetes (during pregnancy).
Symptoms: Frequent urination, excessive thirst, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, and numbness in hands/feet.
Treatment: Insulin therapy (Type 1), oral medications like Metformin (Type 2), dietary changes (low sugar/carb diet), regular physical activity, blood glucose monitoring, and regular medical checkups.

2. What is Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)? What causes it and how is it treated?
Hypertension is a condition where the force of blood against artery walls is consistently too high (≥130/80 mmHg), increasing risks of heart disease and stroke.
Causes: Obesity, high salt intake, smoking, alcohol, stress, sedentary lifestyle, genetics, kidney disease.
Symptoms: Often called the 'silent killer' — usually no symptoms, but severe cases may cause headaches, dizziness, nosebleeds, or shortness of breath.
Treatment: Lifestyle changes (DASH diet, exercise, reduced salt), antihypertensive medications (ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, diuretics), stress management, and routine monitoring.

3. What is COVID-19? What are its symptoms, prevention, and treatment?
COVID-19 is a highly contagious respiratory illness caused by the SARS-CoV-2 coronavirus, declared a pandemic in March 2020.
Symptoms: Fever, dry cough, fatigue, loss of taste/smell, sore throat, difficulty breathing, body aches, and in severe cases, pneumonia.
Prevention: Vaccination, wearing masks, hand hygiene, social distancing, and avoiding crowded places.
Treatment: Supportive care (rest, fluids, fever reducers), antivirals like Paxlovid for high-risk patients, oxygen therapy for severe cases, and ICU care if needed.

4. What is Asthma? What are its triggers, symptoms, and management?
Asthma is a chronic inflammatory condition of the airways causing narrowing and swelling, making breathing difficult.
Triggers: Allergens (dust, pollen, pet dander), air pollution, smoke, cold air, exercise, respiratory infections, and stress.
Symptoms: Wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and persistent coughing (especially at night).
Management: Inhalers (bronchodilators like Salbutamol for quick relief, corticosteroids for long-term control), avoiding triggers, allergy testing, and asthma action plan.

5. What is Cancer? What are the common types, warning signs, and treatment options?
Cancer is a group of diseases characterized by uncontrolled cell growth and spread to other parts of the body, forming tumors or invading the blood.
Common Types: Breast, lung, colorectal, prostate, cervical, skin (melanoma), and blood cancers (leukemia, lymphoma).
Warning Signs (CAUTION): Change in bowel/bladder habits, A sore that doesn't heal, Unusual bleeding, Thickening or lump, Indigestion or difficulty swallowing, Obvious change in warts/moles, Nagging cough or hoarseness.
Treatment: Surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy, hormone therapy, and bone marrow transplant depending on type and stage.

6. What is Tuberculosis (TB)? How does it spread, and how is it treated?
Tuberculosis is a contagious bacterial infection caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, primarily affecting the lungs.
Spread: Airborne transmission through coughing, sneezing, or talking by an infected person.
Symptoms: Persistent cough (>3 weeks), blood in sputum, night sweats, fever, unexplained weight loss, and fatigue.
Treatment: 6-month course of antibiotics (RIPE therapy: Rifampicin, Isoniazid, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol), BCG vaccination for prevention, and isolation during the infectious phase.

7. What is Heart Disease? What are its types, risk factors, and treatments?
Heart disease refers to a range of conditions affecting the heart's structure and function, including coronary artery disease, heart failure, and arrhythmias.
Risk Factors: High blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, smoking, obesity, family history, sedentary lifestyle, and stress.
Symptoms: Chest pain (angina), shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue, swollen legs, and dizziness.
Treatment: Medications (statins, beta-blockers, blood thinners), lifestyle changes, angioplasty, coronary artery bypass surgery, pacemakers, or heart transplant in severe cases.

8. What is Dengue Fever? What are its symptoms and how is it managed?
Dengue fever is a mosquito-borne viral infection caused by the dengue virus (DENV), transmitted by the Aedes aegypti mosquito.
Symptoms: Sudden high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain ('breakbone fever'), skin rash, nausea, and mild bleeding (gums/nose).
Severe Dengue (DHF): Plasma leakage, severe bleeding, and organ impairment — can be fatal if untreated.
Management: No specific antiviral drug; supportive care includes rest, oral rehydration, paracetamol (avoid aspirin/ibuprofen), hospitalization if platelet count drops severely, and IV fluids.

9. What is Malaria? How is it transmitted, and what is the treatment?
Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by Plasmodium parasites transmitted through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes.
Transmission: Bite of infected Anopheles mosquito; rarely through blood transfusion or mother-to-child.
Symptoms: Cyclic fever and chills, headache, sweating, nausea/vomiting, muscle pain, and fatigue. Severe malaria can cause organ failure.
Treatment: Artemisinin-based combination therapies (ACTs) such as Artemether-Lumefantrine; Chloroquine for non-resistant strains; preventive antimalarials for travelers; insecticide-treated bed nets.

10. What is Alzheimer's Disease? What are its stages and care options?
Alzheimer's disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder that destroys memory and other mental functions, and is the most common cause of dementia.
Stages: Early (mild memory lapses), Middle (increased confusion, difficulty with daily tasks), Late (loss of speech, mobility, and complete dependence on care).
Symptoms: Memory loss, confusion, disorientation, mood changes, difficulty speaking/writing, and inability to recognize family members.
Care & Treatment: No cure exists; medications like Donepezil or Memantine slow progression; cognitive therapies, caregiver support, safe living environment, and palliative care in advanced stages.

11. What is Obesity? What are its health risks and treatment approaches?
Obesity is defined as a BMI ≥30 kg/m², resulting from an imbalance between calorie intake and expenditure, leading to excess body fat accumulation.
Health Risks: Type 2 diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, stroke, sleep apnea, joint pain, fatty liver disease, and certain cancers.
Causes: Unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, genetics, hormonal disorders (hypothyroidism), medications, and psychological factors.
Treatment: Calorie-controlled diet, regular exercise (150+ min/week), behavioral therapy, weight loss medications (Orlistat), and bariatric surgery for severe cases.

12. What is Anemia? What are its types, causes, symptoms, and treatments?
Anemia is a condition where the blood lacks enough healthy red blood cells or hemoglobin to carry adequate oxygen to tissues.
Types & Causes: Iron-deficiency anemia (most common), Vitamin B12/folate deficiency, Sickle cell anemia (genetic), Aplastic anemia, and hemolytic anemia.
Symptoms: Fatigue, weakness, pale/yellowish skin, irregular heartbeat, shortness of breath, dizziness, cold hands/feet, and chest pain.
Treatment: Iron supplements or IV iron, B12 injections, folic acid supplements, blood transfusions, erythropoiesis-stimulating agents, or bone marrow transplant for severe cases.

13. What is Depression? How is it diagnosed and treated?
Depression (Major Depressive Disorder) is a serious mental health condition characterized by persistent sadness, hopelessness, and loss of interest that interferes with daily life.
Symptoms: Persistent sadness, loss of interest, fatigue, changes in appetite/sleep, difficulty concentrating, feelings of worthlessness, and suicidal thoughts.
Diagnosis: Clinical evaluation using DSM-5 criteria — symptoms lasting ≥2 weeks; blood tests to rule out thyroid or vitamin deficiencies.
Treatment: Antidepressants (SSRIs like Fluoxetine, SNRIs), psychotherapy (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy), lifestyle changes (exercise, sleep hygiene), support groups, and electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) for severe cases.

14. What is Pneumonia? What causes it and how is it treated?
Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs (alveoli) in one or both lungs, filling them with fluid or pus.
Causes: Bacteria (Streptococcus pneumoniae — most common), viruses (influenza, COVID-19), fungi, and aspiration of food/liquids.
Symptoms: Chest pain when breathing/coughing, confusion (in elderly), cough with phlegm, fever, chills, shortness of breath, fatigue, and nausea.
Treatment: Bacterial: Antibiotics (Amoxicillin, Azithromycin); Viral: Antivirals + supportive care; Hospitalization for severe cases with IV antibiotics, oxygen, and fluids; Pneumococcal and flu vaccines for prevention.

15. What is Thyroid Disease? What are its types, symptoms, and treatment?
Thyroid disease refers to conditions where the thyroid gland produces too much (hyperthyroidism) or too little (hypothyroidism) thyroid hormone.
Hypothyroidism Symptoms: Fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, dry skin, constipation, depression, and slow heart rate.
Hyperthyroidism Symptoms: Weight loss, rapid heartbeat, anxiety, sweating, tremors, heat intolerance, and bulging eyes (Graves' disease).
Treatment: Hypothyroidism: Daily Levothyroxine (synthetic T4); Hyperthyroidism: Anti-thyroid medications (Methimazole), radioactive iodine therapy, or thyroid surgery.

16. What is Stroke? What are its warning signs (FAST), and how is it treated?
A stroke occurs when blood supply to part of the brain is cut off (ischemic) or a blood vessel bursts (hemorrhagic), causing brain cells to die within minutes.
FAST Warning Signs: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency. Also: sudden vision loss, severe headache, or loss of balance.
Risk Factors: Hypertension, diabetes, smoking, atrial fibrillation, high cholesterol, obesity, and prior stroke or TIA.
Treatment: Ischemic stroke: tPA (clot-busting drug) within 4.5 hours, mechanical thrombectomy; Hemorrhagic stroke: surgery; Rehabilitation: physiotherapy, speech therapy, occupational therapy.

17. What is Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)? What are its stages and management?
Chronic Kidney Disease is the gradual loss of kidney function over months or years, reducing the kidneys' ability to filter waste from blood.
Causes: Diabetes (leading cause), hypertension, glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidney disease, and repeated kidney infections.
Symptoms: Fatigue, swollen ankles/feet, shortness of breath, nausea, decreased urine output, high blood pressure, and itching.
Management: Control blood sugar and BP, low-protein/low-potassium diet, avoid NSAIDs, medications (ACE inhibitors), dialysis (hemodialysis/peritoneal), and kidney transplant in end-stage CKD.

18. What is HIV/AIDS? How is HIV transmitted, and what treatments are available?
HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) attacks the immune system's CD4 cells. AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome) is the final stage of HIV when the immune system is severely damaged.
Transmission: Unprotected sexual contact, sharing needles, blood transfusions with infected blood, and mother-to-child during birth or breastfeeding.
Symptoms: Acute phase: flu-like illness; Chronic phase: few symptoms; AIDS: severe infections (opportunistic), rapid weight loss, and cancers.
Treatment: Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) — not a cure, but suppresses virus to undetectable levels; prevents AIDS progression; PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis) for high-risk individuals; condom use and clean needles for prevention.

19. What is Arthritis? What are its types, symptoms, and treatments?
Arthritis refers to inflammation of one or more joints, causing pain and stiffness that typically worsen with age.
Types: Osteoarthritis (wear and tear, most common), Rheumatoid Arthritis (autoimmune), Gout (uric acid crystals), Psoriatic Arthritis, and Ankylosing Spondylitis.
Symptoms: Joint pain, stiffness (especially morning stiffness), swelling, redness, decreased range of motion, and fatigue (in autoimmune types).
Treatment: NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Naproxen), DMARDs for rheumatoid arthritis (Methotrexate), biologics, physiotherapy, joint injections (steroids/hyaluronic acid), weight loss, and joint replacement surgery.

20. What is Cholera? How does it spread, and what is the treatment?
Cholera is an acute diarrheal disease caused by Vibrio cholerae bacteria, spread primarily through contaminated water and food.
Spread: Contaminated drinking water, raw or undercooked seafood, unwashed fruits/vegetables, and poor sanitation.
Symptoms: Sudden onset of profuse watery diarrhea ('rice-water stools'), vomiting, muscle cramps, rapid dehydration, and if untreated — death within hours.
Treatment: Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) — primary treatment; IV fluids for severe cases; Antibiotics (Doxycycline, Azithromycin) reduce duration; Cholera vaccines for prevention; Safe water and sanitation practices.

21. What is Epilepsy? What causes seizures and how is it managed?
Epilepsy is a neurological disorder characterized by recurrent, unprovoked seizures due to abnormal electrical activity in the brain.
Causes: Genetic factors, brain injury, stroke, brain tumors, meningitis, prenatal brain damage, and unknown causes (idiopathic).
Types of Seizures: Focal (partial) seizures affecting one brain area; Generalized seizures (tonic-clonic/grand mal, absence, myoclonic) affecting the whole brain.
Management: Anti-epileptic drugs (AEDs) like Valproate, Levetiracetam, Carbamazepine; ketogenic diet; vagus nerve stimulation; epilepsy surgery; seizure first aid (protect from injury, turn on side, don't restrain).

22. What is Hepatitis? What are the different types and their treatments?
Hepatitis is inflammation of the liver, most commonly caused by viral infections (Hepatitis A, B, C, D, E), but also by alcohol, drugs, or autoimmune conditions.
Types & Transmission: Hep A & E: Fecal-oral (contaminated food/water); Hep B, C, D: Blood, sexual contact, mother-to-child.
Symptoms: Jaundice (yellow skin/eyes), dark urine, fatigue, abdominal pain, nausea, and loss of appetite. Chronic forms may be asymptomatic for years.
Treatment: Hep A/E: Supportive care, no specific drug; Hep B: Antivirals (Tenofovir, Entecavir), vaccine available; Hep C: Direct-acting antivirals (Sofosbuvir) — 95%+ cure rate; Hep B+D: Vaccination for Hep B prevents Hep D.
